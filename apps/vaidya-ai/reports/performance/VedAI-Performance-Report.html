What the scores measure Recall@10 is the average fraction of expected source segments recovered within ten chunks. MRR averages the reciprocal rank of the first relevant chunk, assigning zero when none is found. Higher scores indicate better retrieval.

05 / Methodology and record register

Defined measures.
Reproducible records.

Retrieval sample
The draft dataset contains 68 cases: 48 natural questions, 15 exact-quote targets and five unanswerable prompts. Natural questions cover 12 topics across four language forms and three works.
Evaluation design
Relevance labels were authored during development. Work filters were supplied, and vocabulary was refined after inspecting initial results. Development and validation topic groups are separated by work. The scores describe this development dataset.
Recall and ranking
Recall@10 averages expected-segment coverage over answerable questions. MRR averages 1 divided by the first relevant rank, or zero when no relevant chunk appears. Unanswerable prompts are excluded from both measures.
Answer checks
The 20-case summary records the latest mechanical result for each example across iterations. Criteria include completion, structure, source preservation, translation presence and applicable priority or finding requirements.
System versions
Retrieval baselines precede AI enhancement and later text normalization. Recorded Sol answer checks use medium reasoning. Application verification in this report refers to revision ea1e2f2.
Timing and rounding
Means and medians are calculated from recorded per-case durations. Workloads and environments are identified alongside each result. Displayed percentages and timings are rounded; source files retain their recorded values.
